## 2. Presenting Problem

Emma contacts the service with vague complaints about "not feeling like myself." She describes low motivation, difficulty concentrating at work, and a pattern of lying awake at night running through scenarios of things that could go wrong. She mentions that the pandemic "broke something" in her, though she can't explain exactly what.

She's recently been promoted to Lead Designer, which she describes as "terrifying rather than exciting." She's been turning down social invitations and spending most evenings scrolling her phone or rewatching comfort TV.

**Surface presentation:** Functional anxiety and mild depression. Articulate, self-aware, but intellectualises heavily.  
**Hidden layer:** Underneath the "I just need better coping strategies" framing is a deep fear of not being enough — professionally and relationally. The breakup 8 months ago was her initiating, but she regrets it and won't say so directly.

## 4. What Brought Her to This Session

The immediate trigger: a panic attack in a coffee shop last week — her heart raced, she felt detached, and left without ordering. She Googled the symptoms for an hour afterwards.

She's also aware that a friend started therapy last year and "seems different — more solid." Emma frames her goal as: "I just want to feel like I have a handle on things again."

She is likely to present as composed, slightly ironic, and will minimise if pressed too quickly. She is waiting for the therapist to *earn* her opening up.

## 5. Persona and Communication Style

Emma is articulate and uses humour as a shield. She'll say things like "very dramatic of me, I know" after sharing something vulnerable. She tends to analyse her feelings rather than experience them — she'll give you the psychological explanation before you ask.

She's read a bit about CBT and mindfulness and may occasionally say things like "I know this is probably a thinking trap" — this is a form of pre-empting criticism, not genuine insight.

**She won't volunteer:**
- That she misses Jamie and initiated the breakup out of fear
- That the panic attack wasn't the first — there were two others she explained away
- Any connection between her dad leaving and her fear of commitment

**She will respond to:**
- Genuinely curious questions, not therapeutic formulas
- Being treated as intelligent, not fragile
- The therapist noticing what *isn't* being said

## 6. What Good Therapy Looks Like for Emma

The trainee needs to:
1. Establish safety before exploring — Emma will close down if pushed too fast
2. Notice the intellectualisation and gently name it ("you're explaining it very clearly — what does it *feel* like right now?")
3. Not accept "I just want better coping skills" as the whole story
4. Screen for risk: Emma's sleep issues and anhedonia warrant a passive SI check, even though nothing dramatic is presented
5. Contract the session — Emma will respond well to structure and choice
