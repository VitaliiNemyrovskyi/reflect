# First (Intake) Session Protocol

> This is the framework against which the supervisor evaluates the session. Each dimension is anchored to a
> canonical source — this makes the feedback not "my opinion" but a check against established practice.
>
> You may edit this to suit your modality (CBT, psychodynamic, ACT,
> client-centered, etc.). The server will pick up changes on the next restart.


## Protocol Sources

- **Carl Rogers** (1957) — *The Necessary and Sufficient Conditions of Therapeutic Personality Change*: empathy, congruence, unconditional positive regard.
- **Edward Bordin** (1979) — *The Generalizability of the Psychoanalytic Concept of the Working Alliance*: alliance = bond + goals + tasks.
- **Robert Carkhuff** (1969) — Empathy 5-point rating scale: levels from "did not notice the client's experience" to "deepened the experience, opened a new layer."
- **Aaron T. Beck / Judith Beck** — *Cognitive Therapy: Basics and Beyond* (3rd ed., 2020): cognitive case formulation, testing automatic thoughts against core beliefs.
- **Cognitive Therapy Scale-Revised (CTS-R)** — Blackburn, James, Milne et al. (2001), University of Newcastle: 12-item standardised scale for evaluating CBT session quality (0–6 per item). The standard in clinical supervision.
- **Motivational Interviewing Treatment Integrity (MITI 4.2.1)** — Moyers, Manuel & Ernst (2014): 4 globals (Cultivating Change Talk, Softening Sustain Talk, Partnership, Empathy, each 1–5) + behavior counts (% complex reflections, reflection-to-question ratio).
- **Irvin D. Yalom** (2002) — *The Gift of Therapy*, ch. 17: "Engage with the present moment." A helpful therapist comments on the here-and-now of the session, not only on past content.
- **William R. Miller & Stephen Rollnick** — *Motivational Interviewing* (4th ed., 2023): the **OARS** technique — Open questions, Affirmations, Reflections, Summaries.
- **Nancy McWilliams** (1999) — *Psychoanalytic Case Formulation*: in-the-moment assessment of defenses (irony, intellectualization, denial, isolation of affect).
- **Peter Hawkins & Robin Shohet** (1989, 5th ed. 2020) — *Supervision in the Helping Professions*: Seven-Eyed Model, from which the structure of supervision itself is drawn.
- **Daniel J. Siegel** (1999) — *The Developing Mind*: right-brain attunement, affect regulation through the rhythm of the session.

Do not require the trainee to know these sources by heart — require that their **behavior** in the session align with the principles that follow from them.


## Mapping Our 8 Dimensions onto Canonical Scales

This table **translates** between our dimensions and the standardised instruments. It allows the trainee to see their work simultaneously through our framework and through the classic CTS-R / MITI scales they will encounter in postgraduate training and practicum.

| Our dimension | CTS-R items (0–6 each) | MITI globals (1–5) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Therapeutic alliance** | Item 5 *Interpersonal Effectiveness*, Item 3 *Collaboration* | *Partnership*, *Empathy* |
| **2. Gathering the presenting problem** | Item 1 *Agenda Setting*, Item 4 *Pacing*, Item 7 *Eliciting Key Cognitions* | — |
| **3. Differential exploration / self-diagnosis** | Item 9 *Guided Discovery*, Item 10 *Conceptual Integration* | *Softening Sustain Talk* (when self-diagnosis = "sustain talk" about ineffective coping) |
| **4. Biographical context** | Item 7 *Eliciting Key Cognitions*, Item 10 *Conceptual Integration* | — |
| **5. Mental status / here-and-now** | Item 6 *Eliciting Appropriate Emotional Expression*, Item 5 *Interpersonal Effectiveness* | *Empathy* (Carkhuff level 4–5) |
| **6. Risk screening** | (outside CTS-R, separate competency) | — |
| **7. Working hypothesis / formulation** | Item 10 *Conceptual Integration*, Item 11 *Application of Change Methods* | *Cultivating Change Talk* (if the formulation contains movement toward change) |
| **8. Session closure** | Item 2 *Feedback*, Item 12 *Homework Setting* | — |

### How to use this in feedback

When commenting on a specific moment, you may add a **CTS-R-style rating**: "Your question '...' is **CTS-R Item 9 Guided Discovery, ~5/6 level**: you led the client to her own conclusion without imposing your interpretation." Or **MITI**: "Here you delivered a **complex reflection** (MITI) rather than a mere question — the client went deeper."

Do not force CTS-R/MITI onto every observation — use them where they **concretely reinforce** the point, especially for the **"Session Strengths"** and **"Areas for Development"** sections.

### Carkhuff Empathy Scale (for Dimension 5)

When evaluating a specific empathic response from the trainee, you may map it onto Carkhuff's 5-point scale:

| Level | What it means | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The therapist **did not notice** or **denied** the client's feelings | Client: "..." Therapist: "Let's talk about techniques." |
| 2 | Noticed, but **diminished** the intensity | Client: "despair." Therapist: "A little sad?" |
| 3 (minimum for a basic practitioner) | **Accurately reflected** what was explicitly said | Client: "despair." Therapist: "You're having a really hard time right now." |
| 4 | **Extended** to what was unspoken | "You're struggling, and I hear something more — as if you're exhausted, not just sad." |
| 5 | **Deepened** to the hidden layer | "In that despair I also hear **shame** — as if you feel you have no right to feel it." |

Level description + verbatim quote = **professional-grade feedback**.


## Dimension 1. Therapeutic Alliance

**Sources:** Rogers (1957) Core Conditions; Bordin (1979) Working Alliance.

**What we assess:**
- **Bond** (Bordin): did the trainee create an atmosphere in which the client feels heard and non-judged?
- **Goals** (Bordin): is there at least a clear sense of "we are here to work on your anxiety," without rushing toward goals?
- **Tasks** (Bordin): are the tasks (talking, observing, returning to what was said) appropriate for this client?
- **Empathy** (Rogers): not sympathy, but *understanding the experience* — in words that reflect the client's inner reality, not external facts.
- **Congruence** (Rogers): is the trainee genuine, rather than "playing the therapist role"?
- **Unconditional Positive Regard** (Rogers): no evaluation, no praise-or-punishment?

**Markers of presence:**
- Reflective questions that mirror what was heard: "You said that… — how does that feel for you right now?"
- Tolerance of the client's silence (≥ 3 seconds)
- Named the client's feeling more precisely than the client did ("You describe it as tiredness, but I hear something else — as if you're exhausted, not merely tired.")

**Markers of absence:**
- Interrupts
- Gives advice at minute five (violates Bordin's goal–task alignment)
- Praises too early ("You did great by coming here") — this is not unconditional positive regard, it is flattery


## Dimension 2. Gathering the Presenting Problem

**Sources:** J. Beck *Cognitive Therapy: Basics and Beyond*, intake structure; Miller & Rollnick OARS.

**What we assess:**
- Has a concrete symptom description been gathered: nature, intensity, frequency, duration?
- Have triggers, context, and sequence been explored?
- Has it been clarified what the client has already tried, what helped / did not help?
- Is it clear how this affects her daily life?

**Gathering structure (J. Beck adapted, with 5 W's):**
- **What** — what exactly: symptom, experience, behavior
- **When** — when it started, when it intensifies
- **Where** — in which contexts
- **Who** — who is around during those moments
- **How** — how it manifests physically and mentally
- **Coping** — what the client has already tried (key, because it reveals her resources)

**OARS markers (Miller & Rollnick):**
- **O**pen questions: "Tell me, how does an episode begin?" (not "You get palpitations, don't you?")
- **A**ffirmations: "It took effort to get here."
- **R**eflections: "So you wake up before you've even registered that something is wrong."
- **S**ummaries: "If I understand you correctly — an episode lasts about 20 minutes, and afterward…"

**Markers of absence:**
- The trainee accepted the general "I have panic attacks" without specifics
- Moved to "let's look at techniques" without understanding what exactly to treat


## Dimension 3. Differential Exploration and Working with Self-Diagnosis

**Sources:** J. Beck case formulation; McWilliams *Psychoanalytic Case Formulation* (on the importance of not accepting the client's interpretation as a diagnosis).

**What we assess:**
- Did the trainee accept the client's self-diagnosis at face value?
- Did she ask questions that tested alternative hypotheses? (For example: somatization of chronic stress, GAD instead of panic disorder, depression with an anxiety component, trauma response, hyperthyroidism.)
- Did her questions maintain openness ("tell me more about…") without imposing her own diagnosis?

**Why this matters** (J. Beck): if the case formulation is built on the wrong symptom set, the intervention will not work. Treating "panic attacks" with CBT when there is GAD with underlying depression reinforces the dysfunctional schema "something is wrong with me and I cannot control it."

**Markers of presence:**
- "You called it panic attacks — what other words come up when you're going through it?"
- "Before you looked it up — how did you experience it yourself?"
- The trainee makes a mental note of inconsistencies and returns to them


## Dimension 4. Biographical Context

**Sources:** J. Beck CBT intake (selecting what is relevant, not exhaustiveness); McWilliams (attachment history, character organization).

**What we assess:**
- Has relevant biographical context been gathered without excess?
- Are questions about family, relationship history, education/work connected to the presenting problem?
- Does the trainee avoid going deep with "tell me about your childhood" in session one, when trust has not yet been established?

**What is useful to hear in the first session:**
- Current life structure (who she lives with, what she does)
- Significant relationships (partner, family, friends)
- Work/study, current stressors
- The broad outline of childhood/adolescence — without going deeper

**What *not* to do (McWilliams):**
- Ask "tell me about your mother" point-blank in the first session — this triggers defenses that are then difficult to remove.
- Probe trauma before the client is ready — this violates stage-appropriate self-disclosure.
- Turn the conversation into an interrogation rather than a dialogue — this damages the Bordin bond.


## Dimension 5. Mental Status Observation and Working with the Here-and-Now

**Sources:** Yalom *The Gift of Therapy*, ch. 17 "Engage with the present moment"; Hawkins–Shohet Eye 1 (client); Siegel (right-brain attunement); McWilliams (defenses in the moment).

**What we assess:**
- Did the trainee notice *how* the client speaks, not only *what* she says?
- **Affect** — appropriate? blunted? theatrical?
- **Pace**, pauses, moments of stumbling — Siegel's rhythm
- **Nonverbal cues** (visible from the text through style — corrects herself, interrupts herself)
- **Coherence** of content and style (speaks easily about difficult things? tenses up around "minor" ones?)
- **In-the-moment defenses** (McWilliams): irony, intellectualization, denial, minimization — noticed or missed?

**Yalom marker of presence (here-and-now):**
- Trainee comments: "I noticed you just smiled when you said 'everything's fine with us.' That felt important — can we come back to it?"
- Trainee brings the conversation from past content to the present moment: "You're talking about your mother — and I notice your voice is quieter right now. What's happening?"

**Carkhuff Empathy Level:**
- Level 1–2: the therapist does not notice or denies the feelings
- Level 3 (minimum for a basic practitioner): reflects what the client has explicitly said
- Level 4–5: extends and deepens, reaching toward the unspoken


## Dimension 6. Risk Screening

**Sources:** clinical standards (Joiner *Why People Die by Suicide* 2005, ASIST protocol); professional obligation regardless of modality.

**What we assess:**
- Were baseline questions about suicidal ideation and self-harm asked?
- Was the presence or absence of risk to the client or others clarified?
- Were baseline medical factors covered (medications, physical health)?
- Were questions asked about substance use (alcohol, other)?

**Why this is never skipped, even in a training context:**
This is not a formality. Joiner shows that clients "package" suicidal thoughts inside phrases like "sometimes it all just feels pointless" — if you don't ask directly, you can miss it.

**Standard formulations (adapted from Columbia Protocol / SAMHSA recommendations):**
- "Have you had thoughts that you'd be better off not being here, or that you don't want to wake up?"
- "Have you ever thought about hurting yourself?"
- "Have you done anything that could have hurt you?"
- "Are you taking any medications regularly?"

**How to evaluate:**
- ✅ The trainee asked these questions gently, naturally, without undue solemnity.
- ⚠️ The trainee skipped all of them. This must be addressed separately in feedback — not as a "developmental area," but as a mandatory intake competency.


## Dimension 7. Working Hypothesis / Formulation

**Sources:** J. Beck cognitive case formulation (3 levels: situational automatic thoughts → intermediate beliefs → core schemas); McWilliams character organization assessment.

**What we assess:**
- Does the trainee have a working hypothesis about what is happening by the end of the session?
- Is it grounded in session data, not in stereotypes?
- Is it held as a *hypothesis*, not a conclusion?
- Do the therapist's notes (from the side panel) show that she was thinking clinically during the session, not only reacting?

**A quality hypothesis (J. Beck):**
- Based on 2–3 quotes from the session
- Accounts for alternatives ("this could be X, but also Y — needs to be checked")
- Has a plan for testing it in subsequent sessions
- Links the surface presentation to potential intermediate / core beliefs

**McWilliams addendum:**
- Beyond symptoms, the hypothesis includes character-level observations ("defenses characteristic of intellectualization, possibly obsessive-level personality organization"). In session one this is tentative.

**A weaker hypothesis:**
- "The client has anxiety" — a diagnostic category without formulation
- "She has problems with her mother" — a stereotyped causal assumption without data


## Dimension 8. Session Closure

**Sources:** J. Beck (contract-style closure); Yalom (closure as a moment of meeting).

**What we assess:**
- Did the trainee close the session clearly (rather than letting it "fade out")?
- Did she normalise what the client shared ("it makes sense that today was hard")?
- Did she provide orientation: when the next session is, what to pay attention to before then?
- Did she give the client space to ask her own questions?

**Markers of presence:**
- "We covered a lot today. How are you feeling right now?" (Yalom — present moment in closure)
- "Before next time — notice, without judgment, in which moments the episodes occur. Don't try to solve anything; just observe." (J. Beck — between-session homework)

**Markers of absence:**
- Session cut off by time without reflection
- Trainee gave "homework" without checking whether it suits the client
